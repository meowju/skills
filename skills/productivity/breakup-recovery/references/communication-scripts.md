# Communication Scripts for Breakups

> *The right words at the right moment can prevent hours of regret. These scripts are not scripts in the theatrical sense — they are maps of what to say when you don't know what to say.*

## On This Reference

This file covers the most common communication scenarios after a breakup:
- How to initiate a breakup
- How to go no-contact and hold the line
- How to respond when an ex reaches out
- How to talk to mutual friends
- How to handle learning your ex is dating someone new
- How to respond when someone asks "are you okay?"

Every script is designed to be honest, clean, and self-respecting. The goal is to say only what is true and necessary — nothing added to cushion, nothing withheld out of cruelty.

## How to Initiate a Breakup (If You Are the One Ending It)

The goal: be clear enough that there is no false hope, but not so harsh that you are cruel. A breakup is not a performance review. You do not need to list every flaw.

### The structure that works

1. **State the decision** (not "I think" — "I have decided")
2. **Give one honest reason** (not twenty, and not a list of their faults)
3. **Close the door** (no "maybe in the future" unless you genuinely mean it)

### Key phrases

> "I've decided that this relationship isn't working for me anymore. I need to end it."

> "I don't want to hurt you by giving you hope that isn't real. I'm ending this."

> "This is not a negotiation. I know that's hard to hear. I'm sorry."

### What NOT to say

- **"I think we need space"** — ambiguous, leaves the door open, lets them hope
- **"You're great but..."** — the trailing off tells them the "but" is the real message
- **"Let's talk about what went wrong"** — only if you genuinely want to stay friends; otherwise it prolongs the process

### If you were the dumper and you feel guilty

Guilt is appropriate. It means you have a conscience. Let it motivate you to be as clean as possible in the breakup — not to stay in a relationship you don't want. You do not owe someone a relationship because you feel bad about ending one.

## The No-Contact Rule — Scripts and Rationale

### The rule itself

If you have committed to no-contact, the rule is: **do not break it — no matter what they say.**

"hey" gets no response. An apology gets no response. Even "I can't do this without you" gets no response — because it is intermittent reinforcement, and one response restarts the cycle.

If they text something abusive or threatening, full block. There is no reconciliation path with someone who uses fear to maintain contact.

### Why no-contact works

No-contact is not a strategy to make them miss you or want you back. It is the only condition under which your nervous system can complete the attachment cycle and return to baseline. Every time you break it — even to respond to something that seems important — you restart the withdrawal. The neurochemistry takes approximately 90 days to stabilize.

### Scripts for when they reach out during no-contact

**Scenario 1: Neutral message ("hey", "how's it going")**
> No response. This is not coldness — it is the only thing that works.

**Scenario 2: Apology ("I'm sorry I did that")**
> No response. An apology during no-contact is often an attempt to reopen contact and get relief from guilt — not a genuine offer of repair. If the apology were genuine, they would respect the no-contact and give you space to receive it without the dynamic of contact-restarting.

**Scenario 3: Manipulation ("I can't do this without you", "I miss you")**
> No response. This is intermittent reinforcement. The pattern — withdrawal → reach out → relief → repeat — is what creates the addiction-like bond. Do not provide the reward that reinforces the pattern.

**Scenario 4: Threatening or abusive message**
> Full block. You do not need to respond to fear. "I will not be in contact with you. Do not message this number again." Then block.

**Scenario 5: They reach out to return something**
> "You can leave it outside / mail it to me. I won't be coming to pick it up." Minimal contact, boundaries intact.

**Scenario 6: They claim to be in crisis (not genuine)**
> This is one of the most common manipulation tactics. "I can't do this without you" is designed to trigger your protective instinct and restart the cycle. A genuine crisis from a person who respects you would be directed at emergency services, not at an ex they broke up with. If something feels like a crisis but the pattern looks like contact-restarting, it is likely the latter. Protect your boundary. Direct them to professional support if needed.

### The 24-hour rule

If you are considering breaking no-contact to respond to something that feels urgent: wait 24 hours. Read what they sent. Ask yourself: is this actually an emergency, or does it just feel like one? The version of you that received this message in acute pain is not the version of you who should be making meaning from it. The 24-hour-later version has more stability and can evaluate it more accurately.

## "The Why Did You Leave?" Conversation

If the ex asks why — genuinely wants to know — and you want to give an honest answer:

### Give a cause without a character assassination

> "The relationship stopped working for me. I don't think either of us did something wrong that we could fix — it just ran its course."

> "I realized I was staying out of comfort, not out of wanting this."

> "I wasn't happy in ways I couldn't explain, and I tried to get there but couldn't."

### Do not

- List their flaws ("you were too needy, you didn't support me")
- Use "you" as the subject of every sentence ("you made me feel...") — it puts them in defensive mode
- Make it sound like a negotiation ("if you had just done X differently...")

### If you genuinely don't know why

> "I don't have a clear answer and I don't think it would help either of us to invent one."

This is a complete sentence. You do not owe them a story. You owe them honesty, not a comprehensive post-mortem.

## "Let's Be Friends" — How to Handle It

Both people usually say this. Neither usually means it in the same way. Here's how to be honest.

### If you want to be friends but need time first

> "I think we could be friends eventually, but right now I need space. Not because I'm angry — because I can't actually hear what you're saying without you in my life. Let's revisit in 6 months if this is still what we both want."

### If you don't want to be friends

> "I appreciate that you want to stay connected, but I don't think I can do friendship with you. It wouldn't be honest. I need to move on."

This is not cruel. It is clear. Cruel is saying "yes, let's be friends" and then never texting them back — which leaves them waiting.

## How to Talk to Mutual Friends About the Breakup

### Rule 1

Don't make them choose sides. If you do, you will lose them.

### How to frame it

> "We broke up. I don't need you to take my side or to stop being their friend. I just need you to not update me on their life or bring them up unless I ask."

### If the mutual friend is also their close friend

> "I know they're important to you too. I don't expect you to choose. I just ask that you don't talk about me to them or about them to me."

### If a mutual friend keeps bringing up the ex

> "I've asked you not to do that. I need you to respect that boundary or I'll need to take some space from this conversation."

### If the ex is badmouthing you to mutual friends

You cannot control what the ex says. You can control what you say about yourself. The best response to "they said you were..." is:

> "I don't know what they said, but I'm not going to talk about what happened. I want you to know I have no hard feelings toward them."

This does several things: it stops the gossip loop, it shows you are not bitter, and it means that when the truth eventually comes out (because it always does), your character is already established.

## Responding to "I'm Dating Someone New"

This is one of the hardest messages to receive. Here's what to do.

### Do not respond to the content

Do not ask questions. Do not express how you feel about it in the moment.

### If you must respond

> "Thanks for letting me know. I wish you well."

Then put your phone away and feel your feelings privately. This is grief, even if the relationship ended months ago. It is a final closing of a door. Let it close.

### What to tell yourself

Their dating someone new does not mean they never loved you. It means they are doing what humans do — moving on. Your job is to do the same, in your own time, not to compete with their timeline.

## What to Say When Someone Asks "Are You Okay?"

In the weeks after a breakup, everyone asks this. You are not okay. Here are honest ways to say that:

> "I'm not okay yet, but I'm working on it."

> "Honestly, I'm not great. It's that kind of time."

> "I appreciate you asking. I'm in it right now."

### What NOT to do

- Say you're fine when you're not (others will stop checking in because they think you've moved on)
- Turn every conversation into a detailed account of what happened (co-rumination trap)
- Pretend everything is fine to avoid being a burden

The middle path: acknowledge that you're not okay, don't make it the only thing you talk about, and let people know what helps (distraction, company, or solitude).

## The "I Want You Back" Message — How to Respond

If this comes from the ex and you genuinely want to consider it:

### Do not respond immediately

The chemical state you are in when you receive this message is the same state that made you want them in the first place. It is not a reliable state for decision-making.

### Wait 2 weeks

If you still want to respond after 2 weeks, ask yourself:
1. What specifically has changed — not "they've had time to think" but "they've done X that shows real change"
2. Did they actually change, or did the pain of losing you create a temporary motivation?
3. Would you take them back on the same terms, or are you hoping for something different this time?

### If after 2 weeks you still want to try

> "I need to know what specifically is different and what you are actually offering. I can't make a decision based on a feeling."

### If you realize you don't actually want them back

> "I appreciate you reaching out, but I've thought about it and I don't want to go back. I need to move forward."

## Scripts for Grief Anniversary Contact

On a day that marks the relationship (breakup date, first date, first kiss, the day they left), you may feel the urge to reach out. Here is what to do instead.

### The urge is not a message

The impulse to text on a grief anniversary is the attachment system looking for a way to complete something that doesn't need completion. The date has meaning — it doesn't mean the meaning requires contact.

### What to tell yourself on the day

> "This is my nervous system completing an anniversary pattern. It will pass. Reaching out will not complete anything — it will restart the withdrawal cycle."

### If you need to express the feeling

Write in a journal. Text a friend. Talk to the air. The feeling needs expression, not necessarily a recipient.

### What if they text you first on the anniversary

Same rule applies. They may be reaching out because the date has meaning for them too — but that meaning does not obligate you to respond in any particular way. If you are committed to no-contact, the date does not change that. If you have been broken up long enough to have genuine friendship availability, evaluate accordingly — but not in the first 90 days.

## The Last Conversation Trap

One of the most common post-breakup regrets is the "one last conversation" — the belief that if you could just have one more exchange, you could resolve something, understand something, or say something that would change the outcome.

This is a trap. Here is why:

1. There is nothing you can say that will change the outcome. If the relationship ended, it ended. A final conversation does not undo the ending.

2. The question "why did this happen?" may not have a clean answer. Sometimes people grow in different directions. Sometimes compatibility runs out. Trying to find a fault — yours or theirs — is the brain's attempt to find meaning, and meaning is comforting. But "I may never fully know why" is an acceptable answer.

3. The person who wants the last conversation is usually you, in a specific neurochemical state. That state is not a reliable guide to what you actually need.

The last conversation is not a closing. It is a reopening.

## Scripts for Responding to an Ex Who Is Trying to Make You Doubt What Happened

After a breakup, particularly one where you were treated badly, you may find that your ex tries to rewrite the narrative — telling a story that minimizes what happened, blames you, or makes you feel like you imagined the problems.

This is a specific manipulation pattern. The response is not an argument. The response is silence and your own written record.

### Keep a contemporaneous journal

Write down: what was said, when, how you felt. When you start to doubt your own memory (and you will — this is what they want), read back what you wrote. Your contemporaneous record is more reliable than the narrative they are constructing.

### What to tell yourself

> "My experience was real. My memory of what happened is not in question. I do not need their agreement for what happened to have been real."

### If they send a long text trying to reframe the relationship

You do not have to respond at all. The response can be:

> "I'm not going to relitigate what happened. I understand you see it differently. I'm not interested in discussing it further."

If they continue after that, full block.
