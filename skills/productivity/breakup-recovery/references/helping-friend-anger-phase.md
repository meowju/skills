### The Anger Phase — Embracing It

Anger is not the opposite of healing. It IS healing — the first wave of it. Suppressing anger doesn't make grief disappear; it makes grief wait. The goal is not to get through anger. It is to complete it.

**Anger completion vs. anger suppression:** The research distinction matters. People who suppress anger (Goldman, 2006) show slower grief resolution and higher rates of depressive symptoms at 6-month follow-up. People who allow anger to be expressed and completed recover faster. The difference is not how intense the anger is — it is whether it was allowed to finish.

**The completion model:** Think of anger as a wave. It rises, it peaks, it falls. The problem is not the wave — it is trying to stop the wave before it has crested. Each time you interrupt the wave, it returns. Each time you let it complete, it returns less. This is why no-contact matters: every contact restarts the wave.

**The wave timeline:**
- **Weeks 1–3:** Peak anger. Loud, diffuse, demanding. Physical discharge is the primary tool — your prefrontal cortex is offline for complex meaning-making.
- **Weeks 4–6:** Anger appears to subside. This is not completion — it is the nervous system conserving energy after peak activation. Grief has not yet arrived.
- **Weeks 6–12:** Grief breaks through. This looks like regression — sudden crying, sadness, vulnerability. It is not a step backward. It is the direct result of anger completing its work.
- **Months 3–6:** Integration. Anger returns in smaller waves, more specific. Each wave has a clearer target (*what exactly was violated*). This is information, not dysfunction.

**Somatic discharge:** Anger lives in the body as hyperarousal. Pure cognitive processing during peak anger activation is neurologically ineffective — your prefrontal cortex is functionally offline. What works:
- **Intense physical exercise** to exhaustion (sprinting, heavy bags, swimming)
- **Vocal discharge:** screaming into a pillow, yelling in a parked car, singing at full volume
- **Cold water:** face dunks or cold showers activate the mammalian dive response — the fastest known shift from sympathetic to parasympathetic dominance
- **Punching something:** pillows, mattresses, a kickbag — the goal is physical completion, not destruction
- **Writing without stopping:** 10–15 minutes, no editing, no rereading. Start "I am angry because..." and keep going until the anger shifts. The shift (anger → sadness, anger → fear) is the beginning of completion.

**When anger turns self-directed:** Anger at yourself (*I should have known, I was stupid*) is almost always anger at the situation redirected inward. The reframe: "I am angry at what happened to me, and I am also allowed to be sad about it." Both are true. Neither cancels the other.

**Rage as information:** Your anger is telling you something specific. Ask it: *What boundary was crossed? What value was violated? What did I need that I didn't get?* The answer is usually precise and important. Anger is not chaos — it is your body's way of telling you that something matters.

**When to seek help:** If anger has been the dominant state for more than 3 months, if it has turned primarily inward (self-blame, self-harm), or if it is interfering with basic functioning (work, sleep, relationships), a therapist trained in grief and anger processing can help complete cycles that keep restarting.
