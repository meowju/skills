## The Algorithm's Role in Keeping You Stuck

Social media algorithms are specifically designed to maximize engagement. Content that triggers emotional arousal — especially negative emotional arousal like envy, grief, and shame — generates more engagement than neutral content. The algorithm therefore *prioritizes* showing you content that will hurt you.

This is not a conspiracy. It is a business optimization. The algorithm doesn't know that showing you your ex's wedding photo will ruin your afternoon. It only knows that engagement went up when it was shown, so it will show more like it.

**What this means for you:** The algorithm is structurally incapable of being your friend here. It will keep surfacing the content that causes the most damage, because the damage is what drives engagement. The only effective intervention is to remove the algorithm's access to you — by closing the tab, by unfollowing, by blocking, by not being reachable.

This is not about willpower. It is about removing the weapon from the battlefield.

## Why Rosy Retrospection Amplifies the Comparison Spiral

When you see your ex's highlight reel — their new relationship, their vacation photos, their life updates — you are not seeing reality. You are seeing a curated performance. But the comparison spiral exploits a deeper cognitive bias: **rosy retrospection** — the tendency to remember past experiences more positively than they actually were.

Your brain has been editing the memory of your own past relationship too. You forget the specific难受 of fighting about the same thing for the 20th time. You remember the trip to Paris, forget the 3 days of silence that preceded it. When you see your ex's new highlight reel, your brain unconsciously compares it to your *edited* version of your own past — which is also inflated.

The solution is not to try harder to see reality. The solution is to close the tab. The comparison is structurally invalid — you are comparing their unfiltered present to your filtered past.

## The Bridge Person Wound — Full Processing

The bridge person wound has a specific neurological mechanism: it combines rejection with perceived inadequacy. You were not just left — you were *replaced*. And replacement implies that the new person is better in some qualifying way.

The reframe that works neurologically: *replacement is not the same as ranking*. Your ex found someone new. That doesn't mean that person is "better" than you in any transferable metric. It means your ex was a person who was capable of leaving one relationship and entering another. That capacity is about them, not you.

Additionally: the new person is not the "real" person your ex was always meant to be with. They are the next person in a sequence. Your ex changed during your relationship — and what they learned shaped what they looked for next. You don't get erased. You get integrated into the next person's experience.

## Timeline Panic — The Biology Reality Check

The timeline panic ("I'm running out of time for marriage and kids") has a real basis in biology and a distorted application in comparison. The distortion: assuming that other people's timelines are the correct ones, and that deviation from those timelines is failure.

The reality check: your fertile window is not other people's fertile window. People conceive at 35, 38, 40, and beyond. The "deadline" you feel is your anxiety constructing a cliff that doesn't actually exist at the specific moment you feel it. Even where biological constraints are real, the distortion is in treating other people's timelines as yours.

Actionable: if you want children and you are not on a path toward that, move toward it with more intention. Envy from stagnation is a signal to act, not a reason to freeze.

## The 24-Hour Rule — Why It Works Neurologically

The 24-hour rule works because the version of you in acute grief is not the version of you capable of accurate interpretation. Acute grief activates the limbic system (emotional, reactive, threat-focused). The 24-hour-later version has more prefrontal cortex online (reflective, interpretive, contextual).

The rule is not about repression. It is about sequencing: *let the acute version experience the pain, but give the interpretive version the job of making meaning from it*. The acute version should not be put in charge of interpretation. It is not equipped for it.

## Specific Flavors — Full Rebuttals

**New bf/gf:** They were always going to be there for someone. That it wasn't you is not proof of your inadequacy. It is proof of a path that didn't match. You were not a placeholder — you were real for the time you had.

**Married ex-ex:** You were in love with a specific person, not a generic "marriage." The fact that they're married to someone else means the relationship ran its course. It doesn't mean you failed. Marriage is a social institution, not a achievement badge for love that was "real."

**Pregnant ex-ex:** This is the one that creates the deepest panic because it feels irreversible. Witness the grief: you are mourning the loss of a future you assumed. That grief is legitimate. But the pregnancy is not evidence that your future is smaller — it just means that future is going to arrive on a different timeline and in a different shape.
