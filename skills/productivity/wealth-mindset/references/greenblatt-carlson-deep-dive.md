# Greenblatt & Carlson: The Systematic Investor's Toolkit

*Deep reference on Joel Greenblatt's special situations investing and Charlie Carlson's rules-based wealth system.*

---

## Joel Greenblatt: Special Situations and the Magic Formula

Joel Greenblatt (born 1957) founded Gotham Capital in 1985 with $7M — mostly from Michael Milken. From 1985–1994, Gotham delivered ~50% gross / ~30% net annual returns. In 1995, he returned all outside capital and ran Gotham privately for 15 years before reopening as Gotham Asset Management. As of 2026, Gotham manages $32.6B.

### The Magic Formula (The Little Book That Beats the Market, 2006)

Rank all stocks by two metrics:
- **Earnings yield** = EBIT / Enterprise Value
- **Return on capital** = EBIT / (Net Working Capital + Fixed Assets)

Buy the top 20–30 stocks, hold for one year, rebalance annually. Greenblatt back-tested 17 years (1988–2004) at 30%+ gross annual returns vs. S&P 500's ~10%.

**Why it works:** Exploits temporary market inefficiency where quality businesses get ignored because they're "boring." The annual rebalance forces selling winners (which have re-rated) and buying new out-of-favor names.

**Critical constraint:** Works best for individual stock selection if you can handle tracking error. Low-cost ETF implementations of the quality + value factor capture most alpha with less volatility.

### Special Situations: The Real Edge

Greenblatt's primary edge at Gotham was *special situations* — corporate events where institutional investors can't move fast enough:
- **Spinoffs** — divisional IPOs where the market inefficiently prices the new entity
- **Bankruptcies/restructurings** — distressed debt and equity at fire-sale prices
- **Merger arbitrage** — spread between acquisition price and current trading price
- **Corporate restructurings** — companies shedding non-core assets

Greenblatt: *"You can be right about value but early — that costs you."* The spacing between being right and being proven right is where performance is made or lost.

### The Second-Level Application

Greenblatt's key lesson for personal finance: *don't compete where everyone else is competing.* The Magic Formula works because most investors want "exciting" companies in "hot" sectors. The systematic approach to boring companies is the actual edge.

## Charlie Carlson: Rules-Based Wealth

Carlson's framework from *The Folklore of Money* removes human judgment entirely from investing decisions.

### The Four-Part Pre-Commitment Filter

Before any financial decision, ask:
1. **Is this consistent with my written principles?** (Carlson: write them *before* panic sets in — willpower cannot override emotion at the moment of crisis)
2. **Do I understand the downside?**
3. **Am I comfortable if this decision is public knowledge?**
4. **Is this reversible?**

### The Equal-Weight System

Divide assets equally across 4–6 uncorrelated asset classes (US stocks, international stocks, bonds, REITs, commodities, cash). Every January, rebalance to equal weights — sell what appreciated, buy what declined. Systematic buy-low.

**The behavioral insight:** Equal-weighting naturally does the opposite of what emotions demand — it forces selling recent winners (which feel good to hold) and buying recent losers (which feel dangerous to buy). The discomfort is the point.

### The Cost Math

Every 1% in annual fees costs ~20% of terminal wealth over 30 years. At 10% returns, $1 becomes $17.45 over 30 years. At 11% returns (1% lower), $1 becomes $22.89 — 31% more. Keeping portfolio costs below 0.20% is the most reliable edge available to individual investors.

### The No-Forecasting Commitment

Carlson's system requires no economic forecast, no market timing, no manager selection. The discipline *is* the edge. The person who follows the system through every market condition beats the person who abandons it during volatility.

## Synthesis: Combining Systematic and Active

For the individual investor:
1. **Base:** Carlson's systematic equal-weight + annual rebalancing + ultra-low costs
2. **Alpha layer:** Greenblatt's special situations — spinoffs, restructuring events — where you have specific knowledge or encounter a business in daily life
3. **Behavioral guardrail:** Carlson's pre-commitment filter prevents emotional overrides
