# Peter Lynch: Invest in What You Know

> Full reference: Condensed from SKILL.md v1.109.0 inline section (3,364 chars). Full expanded content lives in this file. Original inline section compressed to ~1,700 chars.

Peter Lynch ran Fidelity's Magellan Fund from 1977–1990, averaging **29.2% annual return** — the best-performing mutual fund in history. Assets grew from $18M to $14B. His edge: individual investors encounter great businesses in daily life before analysts do.

## Core Framework

- **Invest in what you know** — Can you explain this to a 10-year-old? Do you use the product? Lynch found retailers and consumer brands years before Wall Street.
- **The story must hold** — Every investment is a story. Know which one you're betting on and when facts invalidate it.
- **Tenbagger hunting** — Small ambitious companies can return 10x. Lynch held hundreds of small-cap positions because asymmetric upside covers failures.
- **P/E as a compass** — Use PEG (P/E ÷ growth rate) to normalize across sectors. PEG ≤ 1.0 is potentially undervalued.

## The Six Stock Categories

| Category | Examples | Strategy |
|---|---|---|
| **Slow grower** | Utilities, regional banks | Hold; dividends compound |
| **Stalwart** | Coca-Cola, Hershey | Hold decades; raise prices annually |
| **Fast grower** | Small retail/tech | Tenbagger engine |
| **Asset play** | Land, brand, IP | Hidden balance sheet value |
| **Turnaround** | Distressed recovery | Needs catalyst + timing |
| **Cyclical** | Airlines, autos | Buy low on the cycle |

## Lynch's Individual Investor Edge

Institutions can't own companies too small for a $14B fund. You can. You won't face redemption risk or quarterly reporting pressure. You can hold through 3–5 year lulls without anyone yelling at you. Lynch actively leveraged this — he made more money in small-caps where Fidelity couldn't move the needle.

**The two practical advantages:**
1. No redemption pressure — hold through volatility
2. Direct observation edge — walk parking lots of businesses you use. Full lots + happy employees + long lines = company doing well

**Before buying script:** *Do I use this product? Is the company gaining or losing share? Is the balance sheet healthy? Can I hold this for 10 years if nothing happens?*

## The Discipline Checklist

1. Can I explain why I own this in one sentence?
2. Has the story changed — or is the company still executing?
3. Does management own stock?
4. Is P/E reasonable relative to growth (PEG < 1.5)?
5. What's the worst case — will the company survive it?

**The big tenbagger examples:** Lynch's biggest winners: Fannie Mae (10,000%+ return), Ford, Dunkin' Donuts (spotted as a regular customer), Philip Morris. Common pattern: unloved company with faster-than-expected growth. Individual investors spot these because they use products firsthand. The person who works at the mall sees Shein before Wall Street does. The nurse sees which device actually works in the OR.

**GARP — Growth at a Reasonable Price:** PEG ≤ 1.0 potentially undervalued; PEG ≤ 0.75 likely undervalued; PEG > 1.5 you're overpaying even for a good business.

## The 10 Investment Commandments (Lynch's Original)

1. Invest long-term (3+ years minimum) — not trading, owning
2. Understand the business before buying — conviction requires knowledge
3. Avoid companies with too much debt — balance sheet risk kills great businesses
4. Look for consistent earnings growth and strong brands with pricing power
5. Small companies with big growth potential are legitimate targets
6. Don't panic on 20% drops — often an opportunity, not a warning
7. Best time to buy is when blood is in the streets (Warren's rule)
8. Buy companies where management has skin in the game
9. Watch fundamentals: earnings, cash flow, return on equity
10. Past performance doesn't predict future returns — but habits predict future behavior
