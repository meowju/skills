# Purpose Finder — Research Notes & Extended Tools

## Self-Determination Theory (Deci & Ryan)

Purpose and motivation map cleanly onto Self-Determination Theory. The three core needs:

| Need | What it means | What happens when it's missing |
|------|--------------|-------------------------------|
| **Autonomy** | Acting from genuine choice, not external pressure | Resentment, learned helplessness, "what's the point" |
| **Competence** | Getting better at something that matters | Boredom, stagnation, feeling like a fraud |
| **Relatedness** | Mattering to other people | Isolation, loneliness, hollow achievement |

Purpose is not one of these — it emerges from all three being met. The most common purposelessness presentation is someone with **high autonomy and high relatedness but low competence**: they feel free, they have people who love them, but they're not good at anything that stretches them. Fix: build competence. Nothing else substitutes for it.

## The "ikigai" Confusion

The popular "ikigai" diagram (Venn of passion/mission/vocation/profession) is a Western tourist version of a Japanese concept. Actual ikigai (生き甲斐) has no Venn diagram. It simply means "a reason for being." It is often small, daily, and relational — tending a garden, being present for children, doing one's work well. Purpose does not need to be spectacular.

When someone says "I don't have a purpose because I don't have a grand calling," redirect: **"Ikigai is usually quiet, not loud."**

## Ikigai vs. Vocation

Japanese concepts of purpose are distinct from Western "passion/vocation" framing:

**Vocation** (Latin: vocare, "to call") — implies an external summons. You are called *to* something. This frame carries two problems:
1. If you don't hear a call, you've failed
2. The call is treated as singular and permanent

**Ikigai / Ikuji (生き方)** — a way of living, built daily. Changes across the lifespan. Can be a job, a practice, a relationship, a craft. No single "the thing" required.

For the purpose-finder skill: acknowledge the Western "calling" frame is probably what the user grew up with, and it may be part of what's making them feel broken. Then offer the reframe: purpose is plural and constructed, not singular and discovered.

## Flow State Triggers (Csikszentmihalyi)

Purpose often lives where flow lives. Flow triggers:

- **Clear goals**: you know what "done" looks like
- **Immediate feedback**: you know how you're doing, right now
- **Challenge/skill balance**: hard enough to stretch, not so hard you panic

The "Used to Love" exercise in the skill captures interests. The missing question: *at what point in doing these things did you lose track of time?* The answer usually reveals the **challenge/skill ratio** that works for that person. Some people's flow is in deeply technical problems; others in creative constraints; others in helping others directly. Note the *type* of challenge that produced absorption, not just the topic.

## The "Meaning Crisis" Research (John Vervaeke)

Recent work on the modern "meaning crisis" identifies that it's partly a **cognitive framing problem**, not a circumstances problem. People who have everything objectively "going right" still report meaninglessness because:

1. They are using the wrong **relevance realization** framework (they don't know what's actually important, not just what's available)
2. They are optimizing for **extrinsic goals** (wealth, status, image) at the expense of **intrinsic goals** (autonomy, growth, connection)
3. They have **attenuated sense of depth** — treating everything as surface-level, not invested with significance

For purpose-finder: if someone says "everything should make me happy but nothing does," this is often the meaning crisis. The solution isn't more goals — it's different ways of engaging with the goals they already have. This is where the **contribution-first** reframe matters most.

## Extended Quick Scripts

### "I had a purpose when I was younger but I lost it"

> You didn't lose it. You developed a more complex self-awareness that made the childhood version feel insufficient. That's not regression — that's growth. The task now is to find the version of that thing that fits the person you've become, not to go backward.

### "I care about things but none of them seem big enough to build a life around"

> Most purposes aren't big. Most are ordinary and daily. You don't need a cause that saves the world. You need a set of activities and relationships that give you a reason to get out of bed that's more compelling than the reasons to stay in it. That's a much more achievable bar.

### "I don't trust my own desires — they change too much"

> Desires changing is not a bug. It's information. The pattern of what you keep coming back to despite the changes is more meaningful than any single desire. Track the *recurrence*, not the individual desires.

### "I want to help people but I don't know how"

> "Help people" is too vague to be a direction. Narrow it: help *specific people* with *specific problems* using *specific skills*. The world's problems are not all solvable by one person. Yours are the ones you have the capacity and tools to address. Start with what you can actually do, not what you wish you could do.
