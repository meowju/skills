# Ikigai Across the Lifespan — Research Notes

Yukio Kondo's research identifies distinct ikigai patterns across life stages. These stages are not deterministic — they describe common patterns, not rigid prescriptions.

## Stage 1 (20s–30s): "Seeking" — Social Ikigai Dominant

Purpose is often found through work and social contribution. The primary question is: "Where do I belong and what can I contribute?"

Failure experiences at this stage are formative. Research consistently shows that the person who fails at their first attempt at social contribution and recovers builds more durable ikigai in later decades than the person who succeeded immediately without failure.

The key challenge at this stage: social ikigai is inherently uncertain because it depends on others' acceptance. The person who derives all their sense of purpose from being useful to others is in a fragile position — one job loss or relationship failure can collapse the entire foundation.

**The recovery signal:** If after a failure you returned to trying — different approach, same direction — that return is evidence of real ikigai. The person who gives up has a hedonic attachment to success, not a real purpose.

## Stage 2 (30s–50s): "Building" — Non-Social Ikigai Emerges

Around 40, many people report a shift: social ikigai is still present but insufficient. A private practice — a craft, a faith, a discipline — becomes the anchor.

This is the stage where the three-domain model (social / non-social / anti-social) becomes most active. The person who has only social ikigai at this stage is at risk of the "Sunday neurosis" (Frankl's term) — achieving everything they set out to achieve and finding it empty.

The non-social dimension is the insurance policy. The craftsman who has a private practice they return to nightly does not lose their ikigai when the social world disappoints them, because the private practice is theirs and cannot be taken away.

**The warning sign:** Still asking "what should I do" in your 40s usually means commitment was avoided. Direction comes from doing, not from waiting to feel clear.

## Stage 3 (50s–60s): "Integrating" — Anti-Social Ikigai Resolved or Channeled

By mid-life, whatever anti-social ikigai was present — resentment, proving something to someone, revenge-driven achievement — has either been resolved or consumed the person.

The people who find lasting purpose at this stage have usually found a way to channel the energy that was originally anti-social into something generative. The person who wanted to prove their father wrong and became a great builder: the proving became the building, and the need for revenge dissolved because the building was sufficient.

**The key question:** Can you look at your stated purpose and notice whether any of the energy behind it is defensive? Not eliminated — redirected.

## Stage 4 (70s+): "Transmitting" — Generativity and Legacy

The highest ikigai ratings in Japanese studies are typically in the 70+ range. The mechanism: purpose has become externalized.

The person at this stage is contributing to what survives them — mentoring, teaching, passing on knowledge, being present for younger people. This maps directly onto Erikson's generativity concept but with a crucial difference: the Japanese framing does not require the contribution to be "large." It just needs to be genuine and relational.

**The paradox:** The person who focused only on their own achievement in their 40s often has weaker ikigai in their 70s. The person who invested in relationships and mentoring builds a compounding account that pays dividends in late life.

## What This Means for the Purpose-Finder

| Life Stage | Normal Pattern | Warning Sign |
|---|---|---|
| 20s–30s | Uncertain, shifting, failure-recovery cycles | Never risking failure at all |
| 40s | Non-social practice becomes central | Still searching for "the one answer" |
| 50s–60s | Anti-social redirected to generative | Revenge-driven achievement with no redirect |
| 70s+ | Transmission, legacy, generativity | Isolation, withdrawal from connection |

The practical application: where you are in this framework tells you what kind of experiment to run next.
