# Small-Purpose Practice

Daily practice for noticing and compounding small purpose signals.

## The Five-Step Micro Protocol

1. **Micro-purpose audit:** At end of each day, write down one moment you felt genuinely useful — not "changed the world," just "something I did mattered to someone." This trains attention on purpose signals that are easy to filter out.

2. **Start stupidly small:** If your "purpose" requires you to quit your job, move somewhere, and learn something new before you can test it — it's too large to learn from. Break it into something you can do this week.

3. **The identity test:** After a week of micro-experiments, ask: "Do I want to be the kind of person who does this?" Identity is built through action, not reflection. The answer is more honest than any values clarification exercise.

4. **The boredom filter:** If after two weeks of consistent practice you're still not bored, the signal is good. Boredom is information — it tells you the practice isn't connecting to anything real. Move on.

5. **Let purpose be provisional:** Purpose is a working hypothesis, not a lifetime commitment. The goal is to find enough signal to run another experiment.

## Why This Works

Most people who feel "lost" are actually filtering out small purpose signals because they don't match the scale of what they think purpose should feel like. A quiet afternoon helping a colleague isn't a calling — but it's a breadcrumb. The practice builds the muscle of noticing breadcrumbs.

The accumulation principle: small purposes compound. Direction emerges from a pattern of small signals, not from one grand discovery.

## Edge Cases

- **If nothing feels useful:** The audit itself is the practice. Even noting "I noticed nothing today" is data — it's a signal about energy and attention, not a failure.
- **If everything feels useful:** You're not filtering enough. Not all small purposes are worth following — some are just people-pleasing in disguise.
- **If you skip days:** The practice is more about regularity than perfection. Two good weeks followed by a skipped week still builds more signal than zero practice.

## The 2-Minute Version

Can't commit to the full audit? Do this: at the end of each day, ask "What one thing did I do today that I could see the result of?" The result can be anything — a saved file, a thank-you, a delivered meal, a solved problem. The point is noticing cause and effect, which is the foundation of purpose.
