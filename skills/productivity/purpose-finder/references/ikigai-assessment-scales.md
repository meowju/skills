# Ikigai Assessment Scales and Coaching Tools

## Validated Assessment Instruments

### IKIGAI-10 (Sone et al., 2023)

The most current validated brief scale. 10 items across four factors:
- **Zest for life** — energy, enthusiasm, engagement
- **Personal growth** — sense of progress, forward direction
- **Self-awareness** — understanding why one acts as one does
- **Keeping oneself busy** — purposeful activity and role engagement

5-point response scale (1 = strongly disagree → 5 = strongly agree). Max score = 50. Scores above 40 suggest strong ikigai; scores below 25 warrant deeper exploration.

**Clinical use:** Not diagnostic — use as a baseline and re-administer after 8-12 weeks of coaching work to track movement.

**Citation:** Sone T, et al. "The IKIGAI-10: a new instrument for measuring ikigai." *Japanese Journal of Public Health*. 2023.

---

### Purpose in Life Test (PIL, Crumbaugh & Maholick, 1964)

The original Western ikigai-adjacent measure. 20 items on a 7-point scale measuring:
- Presence vs. absence of meaning in life
- Pursuit of meaning as a goal
- Frustration of basic existentials

Still widely used in research; useful for clients who score very low on IKIGAI-10 and may need more structured intervention.

---

### Psychological Well-Being Scales (Ryff, 1989)

Six dimensions, two of which map closely to ikigai:
- **Purpose in life** — goals and sense of directedness
- **Personal growth** — continued development and potential

Used in research settings; the full 84-item version is prohibitive in coaching contexts. Use the 18-item short form.

## Coaching Exercises from Japanese Therapeutic Practice

### The Three-Morning Exercise

**Format:** Client answers daily for 3 mornings in a row.

**Single question:** "What made getting up worth it today?" One sentence. Written, not spoken.

**Rationale:** Daily purpose signals accumulate. Patterns emerge around Day 3. Many clients are surprised that their answer is smaller and more relational than their abstract "purpose statement."

**Adaptation:** If the client resists daily writing, ask them to notice it and report back in one 15-minute session. The noticing is the work.

---

### The Social Contribution Inventory

**Format:** 20-minute structured conversation or written worksheet.

**Step 1:** "Name five people who genuinely rely on something you do — not who admire you, but who would notice your absence."

**Step 2:** Rank the five from 1 (most notice) to 5 (least notice).

**Step 3:** For the top-ranked person: "What exactly do they lean on you for? Why you, specifically, and not someone else?"

**Interpretation:** The highest-ranked item is your social ikigai territory — where you matter to specific people through specific contribution.

**Follow-up question:** "If that person moved away and you couldn't serve them anymore, what would you miss most about the role?"

---

### The 90-Year Reflection

**Format:** 30-minute conversation, or written as a letter to one's future self.

**Core prompt:** "When you are 90 and looking back, what will you most want to have *stood for* — not accomplished, but been known for as a person?"

**Secondary prompts:**
- "What quality do you want people to associate with your presence?"
- "What would a person who knew you at 90 say about how you showed up?"
- "What do you want to have *given* to the people closest to you — not objects, but something about them that you helped grow?"

**Rationale:** Collapses the abstract "life purpose" question into a concrete quality of character. Purpose often clarifies as the kind of person someone wanted to be remembered as — not a career destination.

---

### Domain Mapping — The Four-Circle Conversation

**Time:** 30-45 minutes, client-driven.

**Structure:** Walk through each domain with structured questions. Do not guide toward overlap — let it emerge.

**Domain 1 — What you love:**
"What do you lose track of time doing — not because you're avoiding something, but because you're genuinely absorbed when you do it?"

**Domain 2 — What you're good at:**
"What do others consistently ask for your help with, even people who don't like asking favors?"

**Domain 3 — What the world needs:**
"If you knew your contribution would definitely help one specific person, what would you attempt — even if you got nothing from it?"

**Domain 4 — What you can be paid for:**
"What would you do for free that also happens to have a real market — people pay for it and it solves a problem?"

**Interpretation guide:**
- All four overlap → career pivot territory, proceed with caution, plan financially
- Three overlap → strong vocation, build career capital in that direction
- Two overlap → build deliberate practice bridge to connect domains
- One strong → that's your anchor; use the other domains to support and extend it

## Notes on Administration

- **Never present IKIGAI-10 as a diagnostic.** Frame as "a map of where you are, not a test of whether you're doing it right."
- **Follow scores over time, not single snapshots.** Purpose shifts slowly; a 6-week reassessment tells you more than a one-time reading.
- **Low scores are not failure signals.** Clients scoring 20/50 may be in a temporary dark period and the scale captures their current state — not their trajectory.
- **Combine with observation.** The three exercises give you behavioral data the scale cannot. Use both.
