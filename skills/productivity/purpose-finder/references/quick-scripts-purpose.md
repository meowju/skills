# Quick Scripts — Purpose Finder

*Expanded quick-response scripts for common purpose-finder conversations. The core set lives in SKILL.md; this file holds the full conversation library.*

---

## Core Patterns

**"I don't know what I want":**
> Not knowing is not permanent. What have you tried? Start trying things — the answer comes from living, not thinking.

**"I have no motivation":**
> Motivation is output, not input. Start with 2 minutes.

**"Everyone seems to know what they're doing":**
> No one knows what they're doing. The people who look directed just stopped second-guessing publicly.

**"I'm afraid I'll waste time on the wrong thing":**
> The only real waste is staying in a life that doesn't fit. 3-month trials are not life sentences.

**"What's the point of anything?":**
> Not knowing is not proof there is no point. It's proof you are in the middle of the story, not at the end.

**"I feel like I'm in a rut":**
> Ruts are comfort zones. The fix is raising the difficulty of something.

**"I have everything and still feel empty":**
> Autonomy and relatedness are met, but the competence need is starving. Are you growing — not consuming, but building skill?

**"I feel like I'm not good enough":**
> That's a competence signal. Build it through deliberate practice.

**"I'll never have enough money/status":**
> The sociometer compares to a moving target. Where is your actual enough point?

**"I feel alone even when around people":**
> That's a belonging problem. Usually the answer is vulnerability — terrifying, which is why most people avoid it.

## Advanced Patterns

**"I have a great life and I feel guilty for not being happy":**
> You are not broken. You are experiencing the hedonic ceiling — everything you wanted, achieved, and now it feels flat. That's not a character flaw. That's a law of psychology. The question isn't what's wrong with you — it's what would make you feel capable, not comfortable.

**"I'm stuck in analysis paralysis — I keep researching but never start":**
> You've been preparing to start for so long that the preparation has become the activity. The research is safe. Starting is not. What's the smallest experiment you could run this week — not the best one, the smallest one — to learn something real about what you might want?

**"I'm too old to start something new":**
> Name one person who found their direction at the exact right time and didn't spend years in the messy middle first. "Should have started earlier" is the only argument against starting now — and it's not a good one. What would you do with the next 10 years if you started today?

**"I thought I knew what I wanted but I got there and it's empty":**
> Goals are finite. When you achieve them, the motivation that drove you there typically fades. This is not failure — it's information. The question is not "what did I get wrong?" It's "what direction do I want to move in now, knowing what I know?"

**"I think I'm passionate about X but I never actually do it":**
> There's a difference between consuming something and making something. Passion for watching films is not the same as passion for filmmaking. The question isn't what you love watching — it's what you make when no one's paying you. Start there.

**"I'm just busy to avoid thinking about this":**
> Busyness is a reliable distraction. The question underneath is whether the life you're busy living is the one you actually want. When you have a quiet hour with no obligations — what comes up? If it's discomfort rather than direction, that's information. The avoidance isn't working.

**"I know what I should do but I can't make myself do it":**
> Should is a signal. It usually means someone else's answer that you haven't made your own yet. The fix is not to try harder — it's to ask whose answer this is, and whether you actually believe in it. If you don't, the gap isn't laziness. It's lack of genuine buy-in.

**"My purpose changed after a loss and I don't know who I am anymore":**
> Loss doesn't erase purpose — it blocks access to it. The thing you lost had meaning, and the loss itself reveals the weight of that meaning. The recovery path is not to find a new purpose. It's to find the thread back to the self that had one before. What did you used to love doing, before the loss made everything feel pointless?

**"I feel like I should already have this figured out":**
> "Should" is a fictional obligation. There is no correct timeline for this. The person who has clarity at 25 and the person who finds it at 60 both had to go through their own version of the messy middle to get there. Where you are right now is the actual starting point. What is the next experiment?

**"I want to make a big change but everyone around me thinks it's a mistake":**
> The people closest to you are often operating from the oldest data about who you are. Their caution is real but it's calibrated to the person you were, not the person you're becoming. The question is not whether they're right — it's whether you're willing to act on what you know about yourself even without their support. That willingness is the price of becoming someone different.

**"I keep changing direction and nothing sticks — am I broken?":**
> Changing directions multiple times is not a sign of failure — it's a sampling problem. The issue is not that you can't commit; it's that you haven't found something that gives you enough feedback to want to commit. The pattern across everything you've tried is more interesting than any single attempt. What did the things you kept returning to have in common?

**"Nothing feels real anymore — everything feels like a simulation":**
> That dissociative feeling is common in burnout and prolonged stress. The nervous system has been in protection mode so long that ordinary life feels flat. The body scan — 10 minutes of systematic attention from feet to head — rebuilds contact with yourself. The goal is not to feel passionate. The goal is to feel real. Start with the body.

## Crisis Scripts

**"I'm in a dark place right now / I don't want to be here anymore":**
> I'm not the right person for this conversation right now. What you need is human connection and professional support — someone who can be with you in real time. Please reach out to a crisis line (text or call 988 in the US) or go to your nearest emergency room. We can talk about purpose work when you're safe. I'll still be here.

**"Everything feels hopeless":**
> Hopelessness can be a symptom of depression — and depression is treatable. This is not about finding your purpose right now; it's about getting support so you can get back to a place where purpose becomes visible again. Talking to a therapist is not a sign of weakness — it's one of the clearest signals you're willing to fight for yourself.

**"I think I might be depressed":**
> Thank you for saying that directly. That takes honesty and courage. I'd like to suggest talking to a professional — a therapist can help you understand what's happening and give you tools that make a real difference. Would you like help finding one, or do you already have someone you're working with?

> The purpose frameworks in this skill are not designed for clinical depression. They work best when the person has enough stability to engage with them. The first and most important step is getting support — everything else can follow.

## Additional Core Scripts

**"I tried that and it didn't work":**
> What specifically didn't work? Not "it" — the specific element. Understanding what failed is not the same as knowing what will succeed. It's closer than you think.

**"I can't afford to follow my purpose":**
> Most purpose-driven paths don't require quitting your job. They require 5 hours a week of serious engagement with something different.

**"I'm afraid of failing publicly":**
> The person who fails publicly and iterates is always more advanced than the person who never tries.

**"What if I choose wrong?":**
> There is no wrong choice — only choices you understand better afterward. Every direction teaches you something about yourself.
