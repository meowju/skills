# Natsukashii — Longing as a Purpose Signal

**The concept:** *Natsukashii* (懐かしい) is a Japanese word for the bittersweet feeling of longing for something that has passed or is passing — a place you used to live, a period of life that ended, someone you used to see. It is often translated as "nostalgia" but is more precise: it describes a specific kind of emotional attachment to something that cannot be recovered.

**The purpose application:** Most Western purpose frameworks treat purpose as a forward-facing force — what you want to move *toward*. But the things you would miss if they disappeared — the specific routines, relationships, practices, and places you are already attached to — are a purpose vector that is more reliable than any explicit future goal.

**Why it works:** Longing is attachment that has survived the loss of novelty. Unlike excitement (which can be novelty-driven and insincere), longing points to something that genuinely mattered enough to still matter after the initial thrill faded. This is a higher-quality signal about what is purpose-congruent for you specifically.

**The longing test:**
- What would you genuinely miss if it disappeared? Not what you would miss being *known for*, but what you would miss *being present for*.
- What have you let go of reluctantly, not because you wanted to but because circumstances required it?
- What would you choose again, knowing what you know now, even if it was difficult?

**The insight:** Purpose is not only about what *energizes* you forward. It is also about what * anchors* you when you look back. The things you would miss — especially the things you didn't choose initially but now choose anyway — are a form of longing that is itself a commitment. That commitment is often the truest thing in a person's purpose map.
