# Self-Determination Theory: The Three Universal Psychological Needs

*Source: Deci & Ryan (1985); expanded through Peterson & Seligman's VIA framework and contemporary SDT research*

## Core Model

SDT identifies three **universal psychological needs** that must all be met simultaneously for sustained purpose:

| Need | Definition | When met | When absent |
|------|-----------|---------|------------|
| **Autonomy** | Acting from genuine choice | Actions feel like yours | Resentment, learned helplessness |
| **Competence** | Getting genuinely better | Growth, stretch | Boredom, stagnation, fraud feeling |
| **Relatedness** | Being truly seen | Genuine connection | Isolation, hollow achievement |

> **High autonomy + high relatedness + low competence = talented people who feel empty.** They have freedom and love but no genuine competence.

## The Motivation Spectrum

Motivation ranges from:
- **External** — doing something for rewards/punishment
- **Introjected** — avoiding guilt, performing to avoid self-judgment
- **Identified** — consciously valuing the goal, choosing it
- **Integrated** — fully part of your identity

Purpose lives at the identified/integrated end. The journey from external to integrated is not linear — it requires repeated opportunity to choose the activity freely.

## Competence: The Overlooked Need

Most purpose advice focuses on autonomy and relatedness. But competence requires sustained, difficult effort:
- **Autonomy** is easy to simulate (appearances of choice)
- **Relatedness** is easy to approximate (social connection without depth)
- **Getting genuinely good at something hard** is not

> **Boredom = competence need not met. Anxiety = competence need overwhelmed. Flow = competence need met.**

## The Autonomy Paradox

Autonomy does not mean freedom from constraints. It means acting from inside a value rather than from external pressure, even under limitation:
- The prisoner practicing stoicism is autonomous
- The burned-out executive following a script is not
- Entrepreneurs with extreme constraints who love their work are more autonomous than people with total freedom who feel trapped

## Applying SDT in Purpose Discovery

**"I have everything I should want and still feel empty":**
> Autonomy and relatedness are met, but the competence need is starving. Are you growing — building skill, getting better?

**"I don't know what I want":**
> Try things until you find one where you want to get better at it. The wanting to improve is the signal.

**"Waiting for motivation":**
> Motivation follows need satisfaction. Start with the smallest action that gives you a competence hit.

**"Strong autonomy and relatedness but no purpose":**
> You're missing the third leg. Autonomy without competence is drift. Find something hard enough to get genuinely good at.

## SDT and the Other Frameworks

SDT is the underlying scaffold; the other purpose frameworks fill specific parts:

| Framework | SDT Need Met | Mechanism |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| Career Capital (Newport) | Competence | Rare and valuable skill development |
| Identity-Based Habits (Clear) | Autonomy | Identity before outcome |
| Flow States (Csikszentmihalyi) | Competence (real-time) | Challenge/skill balance |
| Attitudinal Values (ACT) | Autonomy within constraint | Choosing values under limitation |
| Loneliness/Belonging | Relatedness | Genuine connection to others |

The VIA Character Strengths framework (Peterson & Seligman) maps directly onto SDT's competence need: each strength represents a capacity for skilled engagement with life's challenges.

## Conversation Scripts Using SDT

### Opening
> "What three things do you need to feel good about your life? Most people say 'love' and 'freedom' — but there's a third, and it's the most neglected."

### Mapping Needs
> "Let's check each need. Autonomy: when you act, does it feel like your choice? Relatedness: do you feel genuinely seen by at least one person? Competence: are you getting better at something that matters?"

### When Two Are Met but Third Is Missing
> "The autonomy paradox — you can have all the freedom in the world and still feel empty. What makes it feel meaningful is growth, actual improvement at something hard. When did you last feel genuinely good at what you were doing?"

## Research Notes

**Key papers:**
- Deci, E. & Ryan, R. (1985). *Intrinsic Motivation and Self-Determination in Human Behavior.*
- Ryan, R. & Deci, E. (2000). Self-Determination Theory and the Facilitation of Intrinsic Motivation.

**Core finding:** People whose three needs are satisfied report higher meaning in life, better relationships, greater well-being, and less anxiety and depression. The three needs are not additive — a deficit in any one undermines the others.

**The CDC Model:**
- **C**ompetence: Seeking and mastering challenges
- **D**eautonomy (disconnection from autonomy): Acting from external pressure
- **C**onnectedness (relatedness failure): Isolation despite social contact
